# On existential dread

I'm almost at the end of my studies now and have to start figuring out where I fit into the world. Like everyone, I want to find some corner where I can find fulfillment in my job, and ideally, I'd love to be part of the solution. However, me and my generation are coming to an age where we are taking the baton over from the ones who came before us, and we are not happy with what we're being handed. As [Katharine Viner](https://www.theguardian.com/media/ng-interactive/2026/may/06/how-to-survive-the-information-crisis-we-once-talked-about-fake-news-now-reality-itself-feels-fake) very well summarized, we have extremism, the environmental crisis, the information crisis, the loneliness epidemic, fascism, war, capitalism, [AI](https://www.theguardian.com/news/2026/sep/01/if-you-build-something-vastly-smarter-than-you-it-better-be-on-your-side-can-we-stop-ai-from-deceiving-us), inequality, and many more issues being left to us to deal with. We weren't old enough to profit from these issues and too old to be able to ignore them.

All these issues blend together in tightly bound ball of yarn that seems impossible to unravel. What makes it worse is that it feels like I'm constantly aware that these issues exist because social media makes sure of the fact that I'm always just informed enough to know there is a threat but not enough to know whether I can help or that something is being done about it. Anger and sadness are much easier to elicit than bothering to cite some sources or give me hope. My brain is constantly bombarded with negativity. All the issues are marketed as pressing; I have to act now, or I'll be in the wrong, I'll be too late, or I'm part of the problem.

It all leaves me with a feeling of dread. An apathy for any news that crosses my feed, a laziness to form an opinion anymore, and a sense that no matter how I act, I could/should be doing more. The term I have found to best describe the situation is “[Hypernormalization](https://www.theguardian.com/wellness/ng-interactive/2025/may/22/hypernormalization-dysfunction-status-quo)”. First articulated in 2005 by scholar Alexei Yurchak to describe the civilian experience in Soviet Russia, hypernormalization describes life in a society where two main things are happening. The first is people seeing that governing systems and institutions are broken. And the second is that, for reasons including a lack of effective leadership and an inability to imagine how to disrupt the status quo, people carry on with their lives as normal despite systemic dysfunction – give or take a heavy load of fear, dread, denial and dissociation.

It isn't unusual to see how gloomy, ironic, and sarcastic our generation has seemed to become. One way to deal with the general chaos around us is to treat it as a joke. To laugh about how "cooked" we are. Facing our future, being real about it, would require doing something about it. Less and less people have the energy for that. 

So where does that leave us? How are we supposed to move forward?

First issue, most information is always written in a way without nuance. [AI might not](https://www.newyorker.com/culture/the-weekend-essay/why-ai-isnt-going-to-make-art) take over. Not every human is working towards our demise. Headlines and social media posts are often written in a misleading way to elicit a response or radicalize someone. It can be useful to seek out sources that show nuance, that cite their research, and that try to provoke thoughtful conversation instead of making the reader angry.

Secondly, in my experience, always looking at the whole planet and everything in it isn't helping. I believe that our access to everything all at once made us disconnected from the local. We're always being informed about issues we can't have any direct influence over. This can leave us feeling powerless and useless. Furthermore, I believe having all information at our fingertips has made us less comfortable with uncertainty. Not knowing something has become something we are less used to when we can find any (misleading) information in a second. Sometimes not focusing on the solution and just acting in the moment can make you feel empowered again. "In general, if you want change, or to make a difference, you shouldn’t have a fixed idea of what the world will look like in 2050, because that’s disempowering; it removes your agency to be that change. In the uncertainty, that’s where magic, surprise, and delight live," says Simone Stolzoff in an [interview](https://www.theguardian.com/wellness/2026/may/17/author-simone-stolzoff). Experts say action can break the spell. “Being active politically, in whatever way, I think helps reduce apocalyptic gloom,” says Betsy Hartmann, an activist, scholar, and author of [The America Syndrome](https://betsyhartmann.com/books/the-america-syndrome/), which explores the importance of resisting apocalyptic thinking.

A nice reminder is also the fact that people are pushing back. ICE being pushed back in Minnesota or Venetians making a fool of Jeff Bezos's wedding. People are fighting, but "every front in this fight takes on the character and texture of its place." [In an essay](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/ng-interactive/2026/sep/13/end-times-fascists-essay) on our fight against fascism, Naomi Klein reminds us that small choices and small communities are all it takes to fight back. A lot of us are connected to the rest of the world, and not with the local communities with whom we can make a difference. Looking inside your community, you'll be able to join like-minded people and fight for what you believe in. For every rich fascist that is abandoning our planet, there are nameless heroes fighting for good. For every barrel of oil burned, [progress is being made](https://youtu.be/h1jOqyjcO4g?si=RFylk4r7NLcJrKMx) in green energies. 

I don't believe everything will magically work out, and I'm not saying people aren't suffering now and won't suffer in the future. However, this is the time to do something about it. Small acts of resistance and cooperation lead to change. Focusing on the people around you and informing yourself with thought-provoking media can lead you to feeling better about the planet and empower you to face the future. 

Hope this made you feel a bit better. I'm still trying to learn to cope with the direction our planet is going, but I want to wake up every day without feeling the dread of feeling alive. And I believe it starts here.
